Industry Background
IT Service Operations & Existing Operational Bottlenecks
Enterprise Context
Modern IT Infrastructure
  • Educational institutions and enterprises deploy hundreds of diverse computing assets (workstations, laptops, network switches, peripherals).
  • Continuous IT support requests are raised daily by faculty, administrative personnel, researchers, and students.
  • Operations require coordination between multi-tiered support staff, hardware inventory, and manufacturer warranties.
  • Seamless service delivery directly underpins institutional productivity and technical teaching workflows.
Current Deficiencies
Fragmented Management
  • Widespread reliance on unstructured emails, spreadsheets, and informal communication channels.
  • Tickets are frequently lost, delayed, or untracked across busy technical shifts.
  • Lack of clear technician assignment leads to duplicate efforts or unattended high-priority outages.
  • Hardware movements occur without updating user ownership or tracking warranty expiries.

Core Problem
Formal Problem Statement & Academic Motivation
Problem Formulation
The Relational Challenge
  • The absence of a centralized, normalized relational database for IT service operations causes severe data redundancy, inconsistent state lifecycles, and untracked repair costs.
  • Manual spreadsheets lack ACID compliance, fail to enforce foreign key referential integrity, and provide zero immutable audit trails for ticket status changes.
  • There is no structured differentiation between unplanned incident disruptions and routine service requests.
Adverse Consequences
Institutional Impact
  • Prolonged Mean Time to Resolution (MTTR) due to lost ticket historical context.
  • Financial leakage from paying out-of-pocket for equipment repairs that are covered under valid manufacturer warranties.
  • Zero visibility into department-wise IT resource consumption and technician workloads.
  • Compromised audit compliance and operational accountability across technical shifts.

Proposed Solution
Centralized MySQL IT Helpdesk & Asset Support System
Relational Centralization
Unified Relational Model
  • Engineered with a 14-table Third Normal Form (3NF) relational database in MySQL 8.0+ / 9.x.
  • Enforces strict referential integrity with ON DELETE RESTRICT, CASCADE, and SET NULL constraints.
  • Eliminates data redundancy across departments, users, assets, categories, and technicians.
  • Guarantees ACID transactions for all status updates and asset reassignments.
Architectural Pillars
Key Functional Subsystems
  • Decoupled Ticketing: Clear subtype classification into Incidents (outages) vs Service Requests (provisioning).
  • Hardware Lifecycle & Warranty: Asset tagging, serial number tracking, and repair expenditure accounting.
  • Immutable Status Audit Trail: Automatic timestamped tracking of all ticket status progressions.
  • Multi-tier Access: Both terminal CLI and responsive modern web interface connected to MySQL.

Boundary Definition
System Scope & Operational Boundaries
In-Scope Modules
Core System Scope
  • Master department and user records with verified institutional emails.
  • Ticket lifecycle management: categorization, prioritization, assignment.
  • Specialized subtype tracking for Incidents vs Service Requests.
  • Asset inventory with unique asset tags, serial numbers, and warranty contracts.
  • Maintenance expense tracking and total cost calculation per department.
  • Relational reporting: JOIN queries, aggregate workloads, audit timelines.
Explicit Exclusions
Operational Boundaries
  • Commercial payment gateway processing (maintenance costs are accounting logs).
  • Automated machine learning ticket auto-triage (reserved for future work).
  • Direct hardware telemetric scanning or IoT board firmware flashing.
  • Public open internet self-registration (user directory is managed by IT admin).

Stakeholder Analysis
System Users, Roles & Responsibilities
Role 1
End-Users
  • Employees, faculty members, and students.
  • Raise new support tickets with title, description, category, and priority.
  • Classify request as an Incident or Service Request.
  • Track real-time resolution progress and view allocated hardware assets.
Role 2
Support Technicians
  • IT engineers categorized by technical specialization.
  • Accept assigned tickets and update status to In Progress.
  • Investigate faults and record official resolution narratives.
  • Inspect asset maintenance history and warranty coverage.
Role 3
Helpdesk Managers
  • IT administrators and systems leads.
  • Monitor queue metrics, active tickets, and technician workloads.
  • Register new hardware assets, warranty contracts, and vendors.
  • Log maintenance repair invoices and generate cost reports.

Requirements Engineering
Major Functional Requirements (FR1 – FR7)
Ticketing Flow
FR1 – FR3
  • FR1 (Ticket Creation): Users create tickets with unique ticket_no, category, priority, and subtype.
  • FR2 (Assignment): Administrators assign pending tickets to specialized support staff.
  • FR3 (Status Lifecycle): System logs every state change into status_histories with old/new states.
Resolution & Assets
FR4 – FR5
  • FR4 (Resolution): Technicians record resolution descriptions and timestamps with unique 1:1 enforcement.
  • FR5 (Asset Tracking): Hardware assets cataloged with unique asset_tag, serial_no, custodian user, and warranty.
Maintenance & SQL
FR6 – FR7
  • FR6 (Maintenance Logging): Record asset repair dates, descriptions, and expenses with check constraints.
  • FR7 (Relational Reporting): Provide multi-table JOINs, workload summaries, and audit trail extractions.
